## 1. Profile

MiMedx Group, Inc. (Nasdaq: MDXG) is a Marietta, Georgia-headquartered commercial-stage biopharmaceutical/medtech company. MiMedx is **not a clinical-stage biotech** — it is a profitable-in-recent-years, currently loss-making-on-a-GAAP-basis commercial company with multiple FDA-regulated, marketed placental-tissue allograft products (AMNIOFIX®, AMNIOEFFECT®, EPIFIX®, EPICORD®, CHORIOFIX™, and others) sold into two end markets: **Surgical** (sports medicine, spine, other surgical settings) and **Wound Care** (chronic wound management). There is no late-stage, pre-approval clinical pipeline in the traditional biotech sense; R&D activity is aimed at label/indication expansion, new product formats (e.g., EPIEFFECT® randomized controlled trial), and 510(k) regulatory clearances for new product variants. On July 29, 2026, MiMedx announced a definitive agreement to **acquire Sanara MedTech Inc. (Nasdaq: SMTI)** in a cash-and-stock deal valued at ~$350 million enterprise value, which would materially diversify and scale the Surgical franchise if completed.

## 2. One-Minute Read

1. **Revenue fell 35% year-over-year in Q2 2026** ($64.4 million vs. $98.6 million in Q2 2025) as Medicare reimbursement changes effective January 1, 2026 crushed Wound segment sales (-61% y/y to $25.1 million), while Surgical grew 15% y/y to $39.3 million — the business is bifurcating into a shrinking Wound unit and a growing Surgical unit.
2. **MiMedx swung to a net loss of $14.8 million in Q2 2026** (diluted EPS -$0.10) from net income of $9.6 million in Q2 2025; six-month net loss was $25.7 million vs. $16.6 million net income in 1H 2025. Gross margin compressed to 69% in Q2 2026 from 81% in Q2 2025, driven by the Medicare reimbursement rule changes and one-time cost-reduction related charges.
3. **Management announced a definitive agreement to acquire Sanara MedTech (Nasdaq: SMTI) on July 29, 2026** for ~$350 million enterprise value ($33.00 cash + 0.4735 MDXG shares per Sanara share, ~46% premium to Sanara's 30-day VWAP), to be financed with cash on hand plus a new $300 million term loan from Hayfin Capital Management (which will also refinance/retire MiMedx's existing credit facility). The deal received a Federal Trade Commission "Second Request" on October 15, 2026, extending the HSR antitrust waiting period and pushing out the expected close (originally "by the end of the year").
4. **The company cut ~$40 million of annualized operating expenses** via an April 16, 2026 restructuring (including elimination of the Chief Operating Officer role), after Wound-segment recovery from the Medicare rule changes proved slower than expected; management still reiterated full-year 2026 net sales guidance of $260–290 million and said it expects Adjusted EBITDA to approach breakeven for the full year.
5. **Cash fell to $135.8 million at June 30, 2026** from $166.1 million at December 31, 2025 (a $30.3 million six-month decline), driven by a $8.1 million operating cash outflow in 1H 2026 (vs. $19.7 million operating cash inflow in 1H 2025) plus $12.5 million of share repurchases and continued debt service; net of debt, cash position was $119 million at quarter-end, per management's own framing in the earnings release.

## 6. Runway Calculations

MiMedx is a **commercial, revenue-generating company, not a pre-revenue burn-rate biotech**, so the classic multi-year "runway" framework is of limited relevance, but the business did swing from operating cash generation to operating cash consumption in 1H 2026, so a cash-trajectory view is still useful.

- **Management guidance (verbatim framing):** Management did not state a runway horizon in months/years. Management reiterated full-year 2026 net sales guidance of "$260 to $290 million" and said "2026 Adjusted EBITDA is expected to approach breakeven on a full year basis," with a return to profitability expected "for the second half of the year" (per the July 29, 2026 earnings release, CEO commentary). No going-concern language was identified in the filings reviewed.
- **Calculated A, operating burn basis:** 1H 2026 operating cash flow was **negative** $8,136K over two quarters, i.e., an average quarterly operating cash outflow of ~$4,068K. Against the $135,839K cash balance at June 30, 2026, this implies a very long nominal runway (~33 quarters, ~8+ years) if the burn rate held flat — but this calculation is not meaningful as a "runway" because (a) the company has recurring product revenue and management expects to return to profitability in 2H 2026, and (b) the trend is worsening (Q2 2026 alone used more operating cash than 1H 2025 generated), so a flat-rate extrapolation would be misleading.
- **Calculated B, adjusted burn (with debt service):** Adding scheduled debt principal payments ($750K in 1H 2026) and the recurring profit-share payment ($607K), total 1H 2026 cash outflow from operations plus financing-related debt service was approximately $9,493K over two quarters (~$4,747K/quarter). This does not change the directional conclusion.
- **Pro forma complication — the Sanara acquisition overwhelms any standalone runway math.** MiMedx intends to fund the ~$350 million enterprise-value Sanara acquisition with cash on hand plus a new $300 million senior secured term loan from Hayfin Capital Management, which will also refinance and retire MiMedx's existing credit facility (which had $15.7 million long-term debt, net, plus $1.5 million current portion at June 30, 2026, per the balance sheet above). This is a **pending, non-contracted, unclosed financing** (debt commitment letter signed July 29, 2026; deal still pending FTC antitrust clearance as of the October 15, 2026 8-K) and therefore should not be counted as available cash in any runway calculation — it is a material upside/downside swing factor to flag separately, not blended into the cash balance.
- **Conclusion:** MiMedx is **not burn-constrained in the near term** on a standalone basis — it holds $135.8 million of cash against modest existing debt ($17.2 million gross) — but its *operating* cash generation has turned negative for the first time in recent periods, and its capital structure is about to become materially more leveraged (a new $300 million term loan) pending the Sanara deal's close, which was delayed by an FTC Second Request issued October 15, 2026. The debt commitment letter and the deal's "End Date" of July 29, 2027 (extendable to January 29, 2028 for antitrust reasons) are the key dated items to track, not a classic going-concern runway.

Funding sources not counted above: the $300 million Hayfin term loan commitment (contingent on deal close); any Sanara-side cash/revenue once consolidated (not yet consolidated as of the review window).
