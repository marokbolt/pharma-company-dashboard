## 1. Profile

Pharming Group N.V. is a Netherlands-incorporated (Leiden, NL headquartered) **global biotechnology company focused on rare immune and genetic diseases** (CIK 0001828316; Nasdaq: PHAR (ADS); Euronext Amsterdam: PHARM (ordinary shares)). Pharming is not a clinical-stage-only company — it has **two approved, commercialized products**: RUCONEST (conestat alfa) for acute hereditary angioedema (HAE) attacks, and Joenja (leniolisib) for activated PI3K-delta syndrome (APDS). Beyond its commercial base, its clinical pipeline is **mid stage**: leniolisib is in two ongoing Phase II trials for broader primary immunodeficiencies (PIDs) including CVID, with a single registrational Phase III planned; and napazimone (KL1333), acquired via the Abliva acquisition, is in a pivotal Phase 3 trial (FALCON) for mitochondrial DNA-driven primary mitochondrial disease. Lead program one-liner: RUCONEST and Joenja are approved and generating revenue; the next major value-creating events are Phase II CVID/PID topline readouts for leniolisib (expected Q4 2026) and FALCON enrollment completion for napazimone (now expected 1H 2027, delayed from original 2026 target).

## 2. One-minute read

- Third-quarter 2026 total revenues (preliminary, unaudited) were approximately $117.6 million, up 21% year-over-year, driven by RUCONEST ($93.8M, +14% YoY and +30% quarter-over-quarter) and Joenja ($23.8M, +58% YoY); Pharming tightened FY2026 revenue guidance to $380–$390 million (from $375–$395 million) (6-K, October 22, 2026).
- First-half 2026 total revenues fell 6% year-over-year to $162.7 million (vs. $172.3 million in 1H 2025), with RUCONEST down 12% to $130.7 million (U.S. market softness, non-U.S. market exit) partly offset by Joenja up 37% to $32.0 million; the company cut full-year 2026 revenue guidance by $30 million (to $375–$395 million, later tightened in October) and improved its operating-expense guidance by $15 million (to $315–$320 million) (6-K, July 30, 2026).
- H1 2026 cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash and marketable securities fell to $159.5 million (from $181.1 million at December 31, 2025), driven largely by a one-time $12.3 million lease-liability settlement from early termination of the Pivot Park (Oss, NL) facility lease, negative working-capital movements, and higher income tax payments; net cash used in operating activities for 1H 2026 was $7.7 million, versus $12.0 million generated in 1H 2025 (6-K HY1 2026 press release).
- Window event: on September 29, 2026, CEO Fabrice Chouraqui stepped down "with immediate effect" by mutual agreement over strategic-direction disagreement; CCO Leverne Marsh and CFO Kenneth Lynard were named Interim Co-CEOs while the Board runs a permanent-successor search — a material leadership-continuity item to track (6-K, September 29, 2026).
- Clinical and regulatory momentum in the window: FDA approved Joenja for pediatric APDS patients aged 4–11 (≥27kg) on September 11, 2026, and accepted (with Priority Review, PDUFA date January 30, 2027) a second sNDA for lower-weight pediatric dosing (13–27kg) on September 25, 2026; positive Phase II topline data for leniolisib in broader PIDs with immune dysregulation was presented at ESID 2026 (October 14, 2026), with CVID-specific Phase II topline data still expected in Q4 2026 (per the October 22, 2026 6-K, specifically November 2026).

## 6. Runway calculations

**Management's own guidance (verbatim, FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 2.1, "Going Concern"):** "The 2025 year-end balance of cash and cash equivalents, restricted cash and marketable securities of $181.1 million is expected to fund the Company for more than twelve months from the date of this report." No HY1 2026 6-K update explicitly restates a going-concern sufficiency period in the text extracted; the interim statements are described only as prepared "on a going concern basis" without a fresh standalone sufficiency sentence in the sections reviewed.

Note: as with Ardelyx, Pharming is a revenue-generating, commercial-stage company (combined RUCONEST + Joenja revenue), so a cash-depletion "runway" is a secondary risk lens rather than the primary thesis driver; it is nonetheless computed per the skill's required method.

| Basis | Formula | Quarterly use ($) | Quarters of runway | Approx. end point |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Calculated A — operating burn | Cash, restricted cash and marketable securities at 6/30/26 ($159,500 thousand, i.e., $159.5 million) ÷ average quarterly operating cash outflow (1H 2026 operating cash used $7,706 thousand ÷ 2 quarters = $3,853 thousand/quarter) | $3,853K | ~41.4 quarters | far beyond 2026–2030 horizon (figure is not economically meaningful given the company is near operating cash breakeven; see note below) |
| Calculated B — adjusted burn (+ debt/lease service) | Calculated A base ($3,853K/quarter operating burn) plus scheduled lease and convertible-bond interest payments from 1H 2026 financing activities (lease payments $13,894K + lease interest $242K + convertible bond interest $2,630K = $16,766K over 1H, i.e., $8,383K/quarter) gives a combined quarterly use of approximately $12,236K | $12,236K | ~13.0 quarters | ~Q3 2029 |
| Pro forma | No financing closed after the June 30, 2026 balance-sheet date was identified in filings reviewed through October 23, 2026 (the October 22, 2026 6-K was a preliminary revenue/guidance update only, with no financing transaction disclosed) | n/a | n/a | Same as Calculated A/B |

**Latest-quarter run rate sensitivity:** 1H 2026 operating cash used ($7.7 million over two quarters, averaging $3.85 million/quarter) differs materially from the cash position's composition in the period — most of the 1H 2026 cash decline ($104.2 million) was driven by one-time items (the $117.8 million reclassification of money-market funds into cash equivalents is a presentation effect, not a cash outflow; the genuine net use of operating + investing + financing activities was $(104,217) thousand, but $102.6 million of that was a purchase of marketable securities — effectively moving cash into investments, not spending it). Because of this, neither the six-month average nor a theoretical quarter-only figure is a clean sensitizing comparison; flag this as a modeling caveat rather than compute a quarter-only figure from the data available.

**Interpretation:** The mechanical Calculated A/B runway figures (13+ years and 13 quarters, respectively) look very long because Pharming's genuine cash operating burn is small relative to its $159.5 million cash and marketable-securities balance, and the balance sheet also carries $92.4 million (non-current) plus $5.5 million (current) of convertible bonds due 2029 as a financing obligation rather than a near-term maturity. Management's own going-concern language (12+ months from the 20-F) is the relevant conservative anchor; the much longer calculated figures should be read as reflecting the company's revenue-generating commercial base, not as license to ignore guidance.

**Funding sources not counted in the runway math:**
- €100.0 million ($103.5 million at issuance) of senior unsecured convertible bonds due 2029 (issued April 2024) — already reflected as a balance-sheet liability, not a new funding source, but noted because conversion into equity (rather than cash repayment) remains a possibility per the 20-F's hybrid-instrument accounting discussion.
- Access to capital markets, explicitly flagged by management: "Financing, if required, would come via a combination of our strong balance sheet and access to capital markets" (6-K, July 30, 2026) — no specific facility, shelf, or ATM program was identified in the filings reviewed.
- Potential future ex-U.S. partner/licensing revenue from Joenja launches in Japan, Canada, South Korea, and additional EU markets (Germany launched; other EU markets pending reimbursement negotiations) — contracted amounts, if any, are "not disclosed in filings reviewed."
